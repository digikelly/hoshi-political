HB 2418
Permit Review Process Reform
● IN SENATE
● MEDIUM IMPACT
📍 Local Government Committee
Executive Summary

HB 2418 reforms permit review processes for residential housing projects in urban growth areas. The bill establishes clear timelines for permit approvals ("shot clocks") and strengthens project vesting protections. Passed the House on February 13, 2026, the bill now awaits Senate consideration. This legislation directly impacts development timelines and costs in Redmond and other growing Washington cities.

🏛️ REDMOND IMPACT

This legislation directly affects Redmond's development approval processes. Councilmember Melissa Stuart (Planning/Public Works Chair) will oversee implementation. Streamlined permitting could accelerate housing development in Redmond's growth areas, including projects near Marymoor Village Station.

Key Provisions
  • Permit Vesting: Projects vest under regulations in effect at application date
  • Timeline Requirements: 120-day shot clock for complete applications
  • Urban Growth Areas: Applies to residential housing in designated UGAs
  • Administrative Appeals: Streamlined appeal process with deadlines
  • Completeness Review: 30-day limit for determining application completeness

Strategic Analysis

Pro-Development Impact: The bill reduces uncertainty for developers by establishing clear timelines and vesting protections. This could accelerate housing production in high-demand areas like Redmond.

Local Government Concerns: Some cities worry about losing flexibility in permit review. The 120-day clock may pressure understaffed planning departments.

Housing Supply: Proponents argue faster permitting will increase housing supply and moderate prices. The bill aligns with Washington's housing production goals.
