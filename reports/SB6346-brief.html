SB 6346
Washington State Millionaires Tax
● PASSED SENATE
● HIGH IMPACT
📍 Now in House Finance
Executive Summary

SB 6346 establishes a 9.9% tax on individual income exceeding $1 million annually. The bill passed the Washington State Senate on February 16, 2026, by a vote of 36-12 and now moves to the House of Representatives for consideration. If enacted, this would be Washington's first personal income tax and would significantly alter the state's tax landscape for high-net-worth individuals.

🚨 CLIENT IMPACT ALERT

This legislation directly affects high-net-worth clients with annual income exceeding $1 million. Business owners considering relocation or restructuring should monitor amendments that might adjust thresholds or exemptions. The 9.9% rate would apply to income above the threshold, effective January 1, 2028.

Key Provisions
  • Tax Rate: 9.9% on adjusted gross income exceeding $1 million
  • Effective Date: January 1, 2028
  • Tax Base: Federal adjusted gross income minus standard deduction
  • Revenue Allocation: Education and workforce development programs
  • Filing Requirement: Annual returns for affected taxpayers

Strategic Analysis

Political Landscape: The Senate passage with a 36-12 vote indicates strong Democratic support. The bill faces a more challenging path in the House, where moderates may seek amendments to thresholds or rates.

Legal Considerations: Washington's constitution has historically been interpreted to prohibit income taxes. However, proponents argue this is an excise tax on the privilege of earning high income, not a property tax on income itself. Legal challenges are expected if enacted.

Economic Impact: The Department of Revenue estimates $3-5 billion in annual revenue. Opponents argue it may drive high earners to relocate to no-income-tax states like Texas or Florida.

Client Action Items
  • Monitor Amendments: Watch for changes to threshold ($1M may increase) or rate (9.9% may decrease)
  • Relocation Analysis: Evaluate tax implications of moving primary residence
  • Income Timing: Consider accelerating income into 2027 before potential 2028 effective date
  • Business Structure: Review entity structures for potential tax optimization
  • Estate Planning: Coordinate with estate counsel on wealth transfer strategies
