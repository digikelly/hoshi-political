📋 Bill Summary

This bill prohibits local governments from requiring ground-floor commercial space in residential developments within commercial and mixed-use zones. It aims to reduce barriers to housing development by eliminating mandates that can make projects financially infeasible.

The legislation targets a specific zoning requirement that often forces developers to include retail space on the ground floor of mixed-use buildings, even when market conditions don't support it.

🎯 Key Provisions

  • Prohibits local governments from mandating ground-floor commercial space in residential developments
  • Applies to commercial and mixed-use zones
  • Removes "amenity space" requirements that function as commercial mandates
  • Preserves local authority over other zoning and design standards

💼 Business Impact

Development/Real Estate: Reduces costs and complexity for mixed-use projects. Eliminates need to find retail tenants for ground-floor space, potentially accelerating project timelines.

Local Governments: Limits zoning authority in commercial areas. Cities like Redmond with active commercial corridors may lose ability to require retail activation.

Retail Sector: Mixed impact — reduces supply of small retail spaces but may result in more viable, market-driven commercial development.

🔮 Outlook

Passage Probability: 75%

Strong Senate vote and housing crisis context favor passage. House Local Government Committee is generally supportive of housing supply measures. Watch for amendments that might:

  • Preserve some local flexibility for designated commercial corridors
  • Add affordability requirements in exchange for reduced commercial mandates
  • Delay implementation for certain zone types
