SB 6347
Estate Tax Rate Reduction
● PASSED SENATE
📍 House Finance Feb 26
Executive Summary

SB 6347 rolls back Washington's estate tax rate from 35% to 20% for estates of decedents dying on or after July 1, 2026. This legislation reverses the rate increase enacted in 2025, which had raised the top rate from 20% to 35% — making Washington's estate tax the highest in the nation. The bill restores the prior 20% rate that had been in place for years, providing substantial tax relief for large estates. The bill passed the Senate on February 16, 2026, and is scheduled for a House Finance Committee public hearing on February 26, 2026, at 8:00 AM.

✅ IMPACT — POSITIVE

This legislation provides a rate reduction from 35% to 20% rolling back increases from 2025. Clients with large estates will benefit from substantially reduced tax liabilities, preserving more wealth for heirs and beneficiaries. Business owners and real estate holders in the Puget Sound region should welcome this change.

Key Provisions
  • Rate Restoration: Rolls back 2025 rate increase — returns top rate from 35% to 20%
  • Effective Date: July 1, 2026 — applies to estates of decedents dying on or after this date
  • Threshold: Washington estate tax applies to estates exceeding $2.193 million (unchanged)
  • Top Rate Applies: Estates exceeding $11.93 million return to 20% rate (was 35%)
  • Historical Context: Restores rate that had been stable for years before 2025 increase

Strategic Analysis

2025 Rate Increase: Last year, the legislature passed a bill raising Washington's estate tax rate from 20% to 35%, making it the highest in the nation. This increase was enacted despite concerns about competitiveness and the burden on family businesses and farms.

2026 Rollback: SB 6347 reverses that decision, returning the rate to its previous 20% level. This is not a new tax cut but rather a restoration of the long-standing rate that had been in place for years before the 2025 increase.

Political Landscape: The bill passed the Senate with bipartisan support. Republicans like Rep. Abbarno, who opposed the 2025 increase, are expected to support this rollback. The House Finance Committee hearing on February 26 will determine final passage.

Competitive Positioning: The 35% rate made Washington an outlier — even states with estate taxes typically cap rates at 16-20%. Restoring the 20% rate aligns Washington with peer states and removes a significant disincentive for wealthy retirees and business owners considering relocation.
